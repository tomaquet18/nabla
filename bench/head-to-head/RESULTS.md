## What this shows

pg_ivm maintains the view inside the transaction that writes the base table.
A reader that commits a write and then reads the view sees its own write, always;
freshness is zero by construction and there is nothing to measure. The writer pays
for it: it does the view's work itself and holds row locks on the affected group
rows until it commits, so concurrent writers touching the same groups serialise
behind each other. In these runs that cost was a quarter to a half of the baseline
throughput at one client, and at sixteen clients pg_ivm's throughput did not grow
with concurrency at all: it stayed near its single-client figure while the baseline
rose eightfold.

nabla maintains the view outside the writing transaction, from the WAL, in a
background worker. Writers keep their concurrency; throughput grows with clients in
every workload here. They are not free, though: the worker competes for the same
CPU and disk, and while it was behind, the insert and update-fact workloads lost
between a fifth and a third of the baseline throughput. The dimension workload,
whose per-transaction cost falls almost entirely on the worker, lost nothing
measurable.

The price nabla pays is staleness. The view reflects the base tables as of the
worker's frontier, which trails the current WAL position by the lag in the tables
above and catches up at the worker's drain rate. A reader who needs to see its own
write must wait for it. The catch-up column is that wait, and it is large here: at
sixteen clients, ten seconds of inserts took about seventy seconds to apply, ten
seconds of fact updates about two and a half minutes, and ten seconds of dimension
updates about sixteen minutes. At that write rate the dimension view is not usable
through nabla: the worker never catches up while the writers run, and the lag would
keep growing until the slot cap stopped it.

Neither column is a score. They are the two halves of one trade: pg_ivm spends write
throughput to buy freshness, nabla spends freshness to buy write throughput, and
which one is affordable depends on the workload, not on the engine.
